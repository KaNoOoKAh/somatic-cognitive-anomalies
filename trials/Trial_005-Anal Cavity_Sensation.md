# Field Notes: Somatic-Cognitive Mapping
**Date:** September 2, 2026  
**Subject:** Defecation Dynamics & Systemic Somatosensory Progression  
**Category:** Somatic Anomalies / Interoceptive Tracking

## Observations & Context
Tracking was conducted during a bowel movement to evaluate how pelvic and visceral feedback interacts with the somatosensory system when mechanical straining is intentionally minimized. Stool was passed slowly without active abdominal forcing.

## Evolution of Sensation Topology
- **Historical Baseline (Localized Blocks):** Prior logs indicated that sensory feedback during defecation was largely localized to the dorsal (back) region. Distinct structural boundaries were noted where the sensation could not travel, specifically localized as a "block" or blind spot in the lower right back.
- **Current Observation (Systemic Generalization):** During passive, slow elimination, the sensory feedback completely bypassed its previous regional boundaries. The sensation propagated through the entire somatic frame, achieving complete systemic distribution.
- **Cephalic Infiltration:** Of note, the sensory wave traveled superiorly beyond the spinal cord, directly reaching the neck, head, and facial regions. 

## Functional Implications
The transition from a restricted, localized dorsal pathway to an all-inclusive, systemic sensation—extending explicitly to the face and head—points to a profound shift in how visceral reflexes (like the defecation reflex) are broadcasting across the broader autonomic and somatosensory networks under low-strain conditions.
