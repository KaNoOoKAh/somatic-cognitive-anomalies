GitHub Observation Report: Diamond Head Runoff & Substrate Mitigation
Repository: oahu-urban-soil-refuge / field-notes
Entry ID: DH-2026-0829-Cromwells-Hydrology
Focus: Watershed interconnectivity between Diamond Head slopes (22nd Ave corridor) and nearshore marine zones (Kahala Beach).

  1. Problem Statement
Stagnant Acidic Pockets: Sub-canopy soils along the mountain's primary runoff tracks suffer from chemical stasis (likely tannin and phenol accumulation from leaf drop) rather than thermal pyrolysis. This creates a dense, black, sterile matrix where organic matter stalls and root development is inhibited.
Runoff Scouring: Due to a lack of active biological aggregation and high-biology soil structure, these dead zones act as hydrological resistance points. Storm events bypass natural infiltration, resulting in high-velocity surface sheeting that scours the slope.
Coastal Impact: Unfiltered sediment, silt, and acidic leachate channel directly down the drainage network toward Kaʻalawai and Cromwell’s Beach, contributing to nearshore reef sedimentation and poor water clarity.

  
  2. Intervention Strategy & Mitigation Protocol
The Sponge vs. Choke Point Shift: Transitioning upper-slope runoff zones from passive erosion vectors into active biological filters by introducing high-carbon sinks, mineral dust, and fungal inoculants.
Material Exchange Loop: Harvesting heavy, stalled black substrate from the mountain margins and co-mingling it with urban garden amendments (biochar, sun-baked aloe mucilage matrix, and micro-arthropod compost) to neutralize acidity and ignite microbial colonization.
Upstream Interception: Establishing terraced micro-refuges and deep-soaking absorption zones to slow peak stormwater velocity, trap fine sediments, and process road-runoff chemical loads before they reach marine ecosystems.
